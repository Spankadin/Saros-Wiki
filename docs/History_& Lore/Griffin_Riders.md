True [[Griffin_Riders|Griffin Riders]] are bonded companions to their griffins rather than owners of interchangeable cavalry mounts.

The [[../Factions_& Clans/The Aegis Legion/The_Aegis_Legion|Aegis Legion]] maintains the most visible organized riders in modern Saros, but the tradition is not exclusive to the Crown. Riders of any faction normally follow the same dangerous path to [[../Locations_& NPCs/Landmarks/Mountains/Vy'Tungavor|Vy'Tungavor]].

## Claiming a Griffin

A prospective rider travels into the high peaks and attempts to claim a fresh hatchling. Finding an unhatched egg is exceptionally rare and is not the normal route into the tradition.

An adult griffin is not simply captured, broken, and reassigned. The lasting bond begins while the creature is very young.

## Pair-Bond

Griffins bond intensely with their handlers and require regular proximity. Once fixed, that loyalty is effectively non-transferable.

Long separation can cause agitation, refusal to feed, aggression, illness, or attempts to escape and find the bonded rider. The rider is affected less uniformly, but experienced riders describe separation as physically and emotionally distressing.

The relationship lasts for life. A rider whose griffin dies is not routinely issued another hatchling, and a griffin whose rider dies rarely accepts a replacement.

## Growth and Scale

A newly claimed hatchling requires years of care before it can carry a rider.

A standard mature Sarosian riding griffin is larger-bodied than a draft horse and usually spans roughly **18–22 feet** from wingtip to wingtip. Exceptional heavy or tandem-capable griffins may reach **22–26 feet**.

## Saddles and Flight

The saddle is positioned around the griffin's natural center of balance near the shoulder and wing-root region. Exact placement varies with the animal's build and the intended load.

The rider's thighs straddle the torso and the legs rest naturally along the flanks without passing through the wings or obstructing their movement. Sarosian practice does not insist that every rider sit rigidly in front of or behind the wings.

## Feeding and Care

Mature griffins consume tremendous quantities of food. A working roost needs handlers, butchers or fishing contracts, drainage, secure resting space, tack repair, and room for safe takeoff and landing.

This cost naturally limits their number. Griffins excel at scouting, patrol, command travel, and urgent communication, but cannot replace ships or wagons carrying bulk cargo.

Ordinary flight covers roughly **70–100 miles in a day** when weather and rest permit. An emergency courier may cover **120–150 miles**, but such exertion is not a sustainable daily routine.
