    Saros - Distances, Travel, Trade, and Population

    This note is the canonical working source for travel, trade routes, settlement spacing, and population in Saros. Route estimates should be revised here first and then reflected in the relevant settlement notes.

    The familiar continental map is not a modern survey. The version displayed in The Toad was commissioned by Perla Mudridge from an artist who did not possess precise measurements of the entire land.

    For game purposes, the map is:

    • Reliable in general direction
    • Useful for regional distance
    • Good enough to plan journeys
    • Not accurate enough to measure every river bend, coastline, cliff, or road mathematically

    Working map scale: one small hex is approximately 5 miles across; one large hex, composed of smaller hexes, is approximately 30 miles across.

    Recommended Size of Saros¶

    Measure Working Estimate
    Broadest east-to-west span ~220–260 miles
    Greatest north-to-south span ~330–380 miles
    Approximate land area ~45,000–60,000 square miles
    Small hex ~5 miles
    Large hex ~30 miles
    Typical mounted road day ~1 large hex

    This makes Saros a large island or small continental fragment. It is large enough for neighboring towns to be days apart and for regional economies to develop distinct identities. It is small enough for trade to circulate, important people to become known across the realm, and a campaign to cross much of the known world.

    Cartographic Error Allowance¶

    Allow roughly ±15–20% when measuring directly from the map. Winding roads, river bends, mountain passes, symbolic settlement placement, distorted coastlines, abandoned roads, and ordinary artistic error all affect the result.

    The Most Important Geographic Rule¶

    Northern Saros is divided by enormous rivers that function more like inland seas than ordinary streams.

    Goslow, Pyrris, Kylor, Skaggerty, and much of the country northwest of Skaggerty occupy a western pocket bounded by huge waterways.

    To the northeast, the great river descending from The Masys Mountains blocks ordinary movement. To the southeast, another massive river divides the Goslow–Pyrris country from the region of Port Quartz.

    These waterways are too broad, deep, violent, or strategically difficult for ordinary bridges. Obscure crossings may be known to explorers, smugglers, monsters, older civilizations, or travelers using magic, but they are not commercial roads.

    Goslow to Port Quartz¶

    There is no normal safe overland road between Goslow and Port Quartz.

    The principal civilized route is by ship. A vessel:

    1. Leaves Goslow southward through Bishop’s Bay.
    2. Keeps clear of the guarded cliffside waters beneath Pyrris and the restricted waterline approach called Queen’s Gate.
    3. Turns northeast into the great river.
    4. Works upstream toward Port Quartz.

    This is one of northern Saros’s most important trade arteries. Several vessels make some portion of the passage every week. Shipping is not merely convenient; it is necessary to the northern economy.

    Animal Feed and Cargo¶

    Draft animals consume cargo simply by existing. Water is usually obtained along a route; feed requires pasture, roadside fodder, purchased hay, grain depots, inns, villages, or carried supplies.

    Animal Dry Feed / Fodder per Day Water per Day Typical Working Load
    Riding horse 20–25 lb 5–10 gal Rider + light baggage
    Pack horse 20–25 lb 5–10 gal 120–180 lb cargo
    Pack mule 18–22 lb 4–8 gal 150–200 lb cargo
    Heavy draft horse 25–30 lb 8–12 gal Wagon team
    Pony or small draft animal 12–18 lb 3–6 gal Light cart or pack

    Assume a pack mule carries 180 lb and needs about 20 lb of fodder per day when it cannot graze.

    Days of Travel Feed Needed Share of 180-lb Payload
    1 20 lb 11%
    3 60 lb 33%
    5 100 lb 56%
    7 140 lb 78%
    9 180 lb 100%

    Long caravan routes therefore depend upon settlements selling feed, pasture, stable space, repairs, and remounts.

    Major Northern Travel Corridors¶

    The Western Pocket¶

    Kylor to Goslow¶

    Measure Estimate
    Road distance ~45–50 miles
    Walking 2–3 days
    Heavy wagon 3–4 days
    Mounted 1.5–2 days
    Coastal vessel ~1 day in good conditions

    Kylor’s timber, pitch, charcoal, and fish can travel overland. Large timber shipments usually move by water.

    Skaggerty to Goslow¶

    Measure Estimate
    Road distance ~40–55 miles
    Walking 2–3 days
    Grain wagon 3–4 days
    Mounted ~2 days
    Fast courier ~1 day

    Skaggerty is the market center of a wider agricultural belt. Grain from the town, surrounding fortified farm villages, and seasonal holdings reaches Goslow in several practical wagon days.

    Goslow to Pyrris¶

    The High Road converts a short horizontal separation into a major climb.

    Measure Estimate
    Route distance ~35–40 miles
    Walking ~2 days
    Loaded wagon 2–3 days
    Mounted 1 very long day to 2 days
    Griffin ~2–3 hours

    Pyrris depends heavily on Goslow for food, timber, manufactured goods, servants, and visitors.

    Goslow to Port Quartz: The Great Northern Shipping Route¶

    Working water distance: approximately 70–90 miles, including safe passage around the restricted Pyrris cliff waters and the northeast turn upstream.

    Vessel Goslow to Port Quartz Port Quartz to Goslow
    Fast packet or courier boat 1–2 days ~1–1.5 days
    Ordinary merchant vessel 2–3 days 1–2 days
    Heavy barge or awkward cargo 3–4 days 2–3 days

    A round trip in roughly 5–7 days including loading supports regular weekly traffic. The upstream journey toward Port Quartz is normally slower; the return benefits from current.

    Typical Goslow-to-Port-Quartz Cargo¶

    • Grain from Skaggerty’s agricultural belt and other western suppliers
    • Preserved fish and other seafood from Bishop’s Bay
    • Salted food, rope, sailcloth, manufactured goods, tools, alcohol, passengers, and imports

    Typical Port-Quartz-to-Goslow Cargo¶

    • Northern ore, stone, hides, expedition salvage, and goods from Trystn
    • Lilyan metal and mineral products
    • Specialty goods from eastern northern Saros

    Kylor and Skaggerty to Port Quartz¶

    Both routes normally funnel through Goslow because the rivers block normal land travel.

    Route Typical Commercial Time
    Kylor to Goslow by road 2–3 days
    Kylor to Goslow by coastal vessel ~1 day
    Transfer in Goslow Several hours to 1 day
    Goslow to Port Quartz by merchant vessel 2–3 days
    Kylor to Port Quartz total 3–5 days

    A grain wagon from Skaggerty spends 3–4 days reaching Goslow before suitable cargo is transferred to vessels bound for Port Quartz. Goslow is therefore a major break-bulk port.

    Skaggerty to Goslow¶

    The town of Skaggerty and its wider belt of fortified farms and villages form one of the cleanest grain corridors in the western pocket. The town is a gathering, milling, weighing, storage, and wagon-dispatch center rather than the sole producer of every load bearing its name.

    Feeding Goslow¶

    Goslow does not live on imported grain alone. Its staple network combines:

    • Grain, beans, roots, vegetables, and livestock from nearby agriculture and Skaggerty’s farm belt
    • Fresh, smoked, salted, and preserved seafood from Bishop’s Bay and neighboring coasts
    • Fish and preserved goods from Kylor
    • Coastal shipments from southern Saros
    • Imports, specialty food, and emergency stores moving through its harbor

    The bay’s fisheries reduce the city’s dependence on any single grain harvest. Grain remains strategically important for bread, porridge, brewing, animal feed, ship provisions, and the supply of Pyrris.

    Goslow to Pyrris¶

    Every wagon supplying Pyrris must climb The High Road. The capital receives grain, preserved seafood, livestock products, timber, and manufactured goods through Goslow. This expensive supply line helps keep the capital small.

    Port Quartz to Trystn to Lilya¶

    Feed grain, food, mine timbers, tools, and other supplies move inland. Ore and finished mineral goods travel in the opposite direction. The cost of feeding draft animals is built into every pound of Lilyan ore reaching Port Quartz.

    Port Quartz 5,250 2.6–3.9 tons/day
    Karastos ~5,000 2.5–3.8 tons/day
    Soryn’s Crossing 4,800 2.4–3.6 tons/day
    Trystn 4,600 2.3–3.5 tons/day

    Lilyan 3,800 1.9–2.9 tons/day
    Ani 3,000 1.5–2.2 tons/day
    Mysha 2,500 1.2–1.9 tons/day
    Kylor 2,400 1.2–1.8 tons/day

    Recommended Total Population¶

    Population Group Working Estimate
    Named settlements and strongholds ~73,600
    Secondary market towns and fortified towns ~42,000
    Villages, farms, and agricultural holds ~154,400
    Hidden enclaves, delves, pirate ports, nomads, islands, and remote camps ~30,000
    Working total ~300,000

    A plausible present range remains 280,000–320,000. Council counts are lower because hidden, independent, mobile, and remote peoples are difficult or politically inconvenient to enumerate.

    The working demographic model remains approximately 85% human, with dwarves, halflings, elves, orcs, mixed communities, and other peoples forming the remainder. See Sarosian Timeline for the current breakdown and historical growth model.

    The named settlements contain about one quarter of the population. Most other Sarosians still cluster in fortified farm villages, feed stations, logging camps, mines, ferries, fishing villages, temple estates, and road forts rather than isolated homesteads.

    Water Is the Backbone of Trade¶

    The practical commercial map is built around:

    • Bishop’s Bay
    • The Goslow–Port Quartz shipping route
    • The rivers connected to Port Quartz
    • The lake crossing between Albyrt and Isoltide
    • Southern river traffic through Soryn’s Crossing
    • Eastern coastal routes through Rore and Ari
    • Military sea lanes around Grymswatch and Karastos

    This explains why pirates remain politically important and why a modest port can be strategically powerful.

    Strategic Choke Points¶

    Choke Point Why It Matters
    Goslow harbor Western freight, Bishop’s Bay, Pyrris supply, and Port Quartz shipping
    The High Road Primary public supply route into Pyrris
    Goslow–Port Quartz river route Main civilized trade connection between northern west and east
    Port Quartz Converts waterborne cargo into road cargo and vice versa
    Trystn Splits freight toward Lilya, Rore, and Port Quartz
    Westwater Ferry Access to the unsettled Masys side of the western river
    Albyrt–Isoltide lake crossing Twin-city passenger and bulk trade
    Soryn’s Crossing Southern overland and river freight redistribution
    Grymswatch Military control of dangerous southern sea lanes
    The Wyrm Scar Makes simple north–south overland travel unrealistic

    Griffin Travel¶

    Griffin Riders make the Aegis Legion strategically mobile without replacing ships or roads.

    • Ordinary movement: 70–100 miles/day
    • Emergency courier: 120–150 miles/day
    • Light cargo only
    • Weather, landing sites, food, rest, handlers, and specialized care still matter

    Their greatest strategic advantage is the ability to ignore rivers. A griffin can cross in hours a barrier that forces a merchant into a multi-day shipping route. Their limited cargo and tremendous upkeep keep them from replacing boats carrying grain.

    Settlement Spacing¶

    Separation Likely Relationship
    Under 20 miles Same-day local market zone
    20–40 miles Close neighbor; routine local exchange
    40–70 miles Distinct town economy; 2–4 ordinary travel days
    70–120 miles Regional trade route
    120–200 miles Long-distance commerce; staging towns matter
    200+ miles Major journey for ordinary people

    A river may matter more than fifty miles. A mountain may matter more than one hundred. A ship may make two distant ports effectively closer than two nearby inland settlements.

    Quick GM Travel Formula¶

    Road¶

    • Walking: route miles ÷ 20
    • Heavy wagon: route miles ÷ 15
    • Mounted: route miles ÷ 30
    • Add 15–25% calendar delay for an ordinary journey.

    Wilderness¶

    1. Measure the approximate straight-line distance.
    2. Add 10–30% for route finding.
    3. Divide by 12–15 mi/day in open country, 8–12 in forest or hills, or 5–8 in mountain or bog.
    4. Add river-crossing and searching delays separately.

    Ship¶

    • Coastal or favorable water: route miles ÷ 70–90
    • Ordinary upstream river travel: route miles ÷ 15–25
    • Downstream river travel: route miles ÷ 30–50
    • Add loading, weather, current, and port delays.

    Working Canon Summary¶

    Saros uses a working scale of approximately 5 miles per small hex and 30 miles per large hex.

    The known land is roughly 220–260 miles across and 330–380 miles north-to-south, but the familiar map is intentionally approximate.

    Named settlements and strongholds contain about 73,600 people. The working total population is approximately 300,000.

    Skaggerty is a 1,200-person agricultural market town serving a much larger belt of farms and fortified villages.

    Goslow, Pyrris, Kylor, Skaggerty, and the western northern country are separated from Port Quartz by enormous rivers. There is no ordinary safe commercial road between Goslow and Port Quartz.

    The normal Goslow–Port Quartz connection is a 70–90 mile water route.

    Goslow is fed by grain and produce, but also by extensive seafood from Bishop’s Bay and neighboring coasts.

    Saros should feel like fortified islands of civilization connected by roads, rivers, ports, ferries, and sea lanes through an older and more dangerous world.
